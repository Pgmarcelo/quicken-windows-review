# Calculation and interpretation rules

## Spending versus cash capacity

Use positive magnitudes in the review after documenting source signs. Internal transfers between owned accounts are neither income nor economic spending. Credit-card purchases are spending; paying the card is a transfer, with interest/fees counted separately. If the receiving account is absent, classify a suspected transfer as uncertain rather than deleting it. Refunds reduce related spending when traceable.

Loan principal reduces a liability; interest is an expense. For a monthly available-cash view, subtract required debt service once, including principal. Savings/investment contributions are asset transfers; show them separately as cash commitments. Securities sales are not salary, and proceeds are not equivalent to gains. Avoid counting both sale proceeds and transfer of those proceeds as new income.

Define the cash boundary and state whether contributions are already included. Reconcile movements between cash, investments, and liabilities. Distinguish before-discretionary-saving capacity from after-planned-contributions cash. If total expenses already include minimum debt payments, extra debt capacity = income - those expenses - additional commitments not already included. Do not subtract minimums again.

Example: CAD 6,000 income and CAD 4,500 outflows already containing CAD 500 required debt service leaves CAD 1,500 before other uncounted commitments. If those outflows also contain CAD 400 own-savings transfer, capacity before that saving is CAD 1,900 and after it is CAD 1,500. This is a cash view; economic spending must separately remove transfers and principal and include credit-card purchases exactly once. Missing scope prevents a definitive spending total.

Compare complete periods with like periods; label month-to-date and seasonal effects. Savings rate needs an explicit numerator/denominator and treatment of payroll retirement deductions. Unknown gross pay or employer contributions cannot be inferred from net deposits. Reserve months = eligible accessible cash / stated essential monthly cash needs; show exclusions and accessibility restrictions.

## Debt scenarios

Require balance/date, rate, minimum, extra-payment budget, and payment timing. State fixed/variable rates, interest convention, minimum-payment changes, fees, and prepayment assumptions. APR/12 is a simplified monthly approximation, not universally a Canadian mortgage calculation. Use lender conventions for precise mortgage projections; otherwise label approximate.

Apply interest and payments in the stated order, cap final payment at balance plus accrued interest, and carry freed payments to the next debt. Never report negative balances or payoff where payments do not cover interest. Report total interest, payoff date, payment schedule, and cash buffer. A debt principal payment is not spending reduction. Lower payments from refinancing are not automatically lower lifetime cost; compare fees, term, total interest, and rate sensitivity.

## Investments and Canadian context

Use market value for net worth; cost basis is a separate metric. Do not add security values to an account total already containing them or add the same brokerage cash twice. Account wrapper (RRSP, TFSA, FHSA, RESP, taxable, pension) differs from asset class. Registered balances are not necessarily equally accessible or equal after-tax values. Keep any estimated tax-adjusted view separate.

Exposure weights need a stated denominator; missing classifications remain unknown. Fund look-through requires reliable holdings dates and weights. Include employer shares and vested RSUs in concentration where present, without duplicating brokerage positions. Unvested RSUs/options are contingent future compensation: show separately from owned net worth; distinguish gross grant estimates, vesting taxes, sale proceeds, and deposited shares. Do not recommend trades using stale quotes as executable prices.

Investment return is not change in balance divided by beginning balance when external flows exist. Use Quicken's performance report/method where available and reconciled. Calculate money-weighted return only with complete dated flows and terminal value; explain multiple/no-root cases. Time-weighted return requires valuations around external flows. Otherwise report change and contributions separately, with performance unavailable.

Canadian taxable ACB may require pooled identical-property holdings across accounts, CAD conversion, reinvested distributions, return of capital, fees, and corporate actions. Flag missing records and superficial-loss considerations; verify applicable CRA guidance before tax recommendations. Do not substitute a broker's lot gain for Canadian taxable gain. Verify contribution room from user-provided current records and recent transactions before proposing registered-account amounts; never infer room from balances.

## Net worth and currencies

Net worth = owned assets - liabilities using consistent ownership shares and dates. Disclose excluded accounts, estimated property values, stale prices, and liabilities missing from exports. Do not present mixed-date totals as an exact single-date snapshot. Separate owned net worth, liquid reserve, and contingent compensation.

Never add CAD and USD directly. For snapshots use disclosed as-of FX; for historical flows use an appropriate dated conversion or documented approximation. Preserve original currency, rate, rate date/source, and CAD value. Do not reconvert report values already expressed in CAD. If FX is unavailable, show currency subtotals and withhold consolidated values. For investment changes distinguish FX from local-currency performance when data permits.

## Improvement estimates

Tie each opportunity to evidence: excess recurring fees, avoidable subscriptions, high interest, cash shortfalls, concentration, stale categorization, or goal funding gaps. Recurrence inferred from repeated charges is a candidate, not proof a service is unused. Present savings as estimates before taxes/fees where appropriate. Do not count one subscription reduction both individually and inside a broader category-cut total.

Separate controllable savings from uncertain investment outcomes. For forecasts disclose inflation, returns, tax, FX, contributions, and horizon; show sensitivity rather than a guaranteed outcome. Obtain current authoritative support for consequential legal/tax/financial guidance, with links and retrieval dates. Keep educational analysis proportional to available facts.
